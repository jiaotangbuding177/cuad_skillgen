# SKILL.md: Revenue and Commercial Terms Contract Review

## Skill Overview

This skill enables the systematic review of contracts to identify and analyze four key revenue and commercial terms categories: Revenue/Profit Sharing, Price Restrictions, Minimum Commitments, and Volume Restrictions. The reviewer examines contractual provisions governing how parties share financial returns, control pricing, commit to minimum purchases, and handle usage thresholds.

The skill is essential for understanding the financial obligations, risks, and constraints embedded in commercial agreements, particularly in licensing, distribution, co-promotion, endorsement, and service agreements.

---

## Review Categories and Steps

### Category 1: Revenue/Profit Sharing

**Question:** Is one party required to share revenue or profit with the counterparty for any technology, goods, or services?

**Answer Format:** Yes/No

#### What to Look For

Revenue sharing provisions typically appear in:
- **Royalty clauses** - Percentage-based payments on sales or revenue
- **Commission structures** - Payments based on transactions generated
- **Profit split arrangements** - Division of margins or net profits
- **Revenue share percentages** - Explicit sharing of gross or net revenue
- **Bounty or referral fees** - Payments for customer introductions
- **Co-branding revenue splits** - Division of revenue from jointly marketed products

#### Key Contractual Patterns Observed

**Pattern 1: Percentage-based royalties on sales**
- *Example from Glu Mobile/Fox agreement:* "Fox shall earn... Royalties at the rate of [***]% of Licensee's Gross Receipts"
- *Example from MusclePharm agreement:* "MusclePharm shall pay Lender a royalty... of 10% on Net Sales of Licensed Products"

**Pattern 2: Tiered revenue sharing based on thresholds**
- *Example from N2K/Excite agreement:* Revenue sharing kicks in only after Total Revenue exceeds a "Revenue Floor," with the excess shared at a specified percentage
- *Example from Kubient/AP agreement:* Different revenue share percentages apply "Below Threshold" vs. "Above Threshold" for each revenue type

**Pattern 3: Profit margin sharing**
- *Example from Sekisui/Qualigen agreement:* Pricing based on "Available Margin" with specific percentage splits (90%/70%/65% to Sekisui at different phases)

**Pattern 4: Per-unit or fixed-amount sharing**
- *Example from Gridiron BioNutrients agreement:* "$0.05 per Unit sold of Licensed Products" as a donation/royalty
- *Example from iShip/MBE agreement:* "Basic Fee" of [***] per package shipped

**Pattern 5: Guaranteed minimum royalties**
- *Example from MusclePharm agreement:* "Lender shall be entitled to receive a guaranteed minimum royalty for each Contract Year"

#### Review Steps

1. **Identify all payment provisions** - Scan for sections titled "Royalties," "Compensation," "Revenue Share," "Fees," or "Payments"
2. **Determine the sharing basis** - Is it gross revenue, net sales, profit margin, or per-unit?
3. **Check for deductions** - What can be deducted before calculating the share? (e.g., "less only monies actually retained by the CSPs")
4. **Look for minimum guarantees** - Are there guaranteed minimum payments regardless of actual revenue?
5. **Identify tier structures** - Does the sharing percentage change based on volume or time?
6. **Check for cross-collateralization** - Can overpayments on one product offset shortfalls on another?

### Category 2: Price Restrictions

**Question:** Is there a restriction on the ability of a party to raise or reduce prices of technology, goods, or services provided?

**Answer Format:** Yes/No

#### What to Look For

Price restrictions appear in:
- **Most favored nation (MFN) clauses** - Requiring equal or better pricing than offered to others
- **Price approval rights** - One party must approve price changes
- **Price caps or floors** - Maximum or minimum prices
- **Fixed pricing periods** - Prices locked for specified durations
- **Formula-based pricing** - Prices determined by formula rather than discretion
- **Government or regulatory pricing** - Prices subject to government approval
- **Anti-price discrimination provisions** - Restrictions on offering different prices to different customers

#### Key Contractual Patterns Observed

**Pattern 1: Formula-based pricing with limited discretion**
- *Example from Sekisui/Qualigen agreement:* "The price that Sekisui shall pay for the Products shall be established separately... based upon a formula intended to ensure that Sekisui will receive 90% of the total Available Margin"
- Prices are "fixed (subject to a later lookback true up) for each respective prospective six month period"

**Pattern 2: Customer pricing set by one party**
- *Example from Sekisui/Qualigen agreement:* "Sekisui shall set the customer selling prices in good faith and in a commercially reasonable manner" - This gives Sekisui pricing discretion but with a reasonableness constraint

**Pattern 3: Pricing subject to regulatory approval**
- *Example from Array/Ono agreement:* References to "Pricing and Reimbursement Approval" from Governmental Authorities
- *Example from STW Resources/COFS agreement:* Royalty of "seventeen percent (17%) of the price STW receives per 1,000 gallons" - price is determined by STW's sales to third parties

**Pattern 4: No explicit price restrictions (common in many agreements)**
- Many agreements reviewed do not contain explicit price restrictions, instead focusing on revenue sharing or royalty calculations based on actual sales prices

#### Review Steps

1. **Examine pricing provisions** - Look for sections on "Price," "Pricing," "Fees," or "Compensation"
2. **Check for approval requirements** - Does one party need to approve the other's price changes?
3. **Identify pricing formulas** - Are prices determined by a fixed formula or at a party's discretion?
4. **Look for MFN clauses** - Does the agreement require pricing parity with other customers?
5. **Check for regulatory constraints** - Are prices subject to government or regulatory approval?
6. **Review for price change notice periods** - Are there requirements to notify before changing prices?

### Category 3: Minimum Commitment

**Question:** Is there a minimum order size or minimum amount or units per time period that one party must buy from the counterparty under the contract?

**Answer Format:** Yes/No

#### What to Look For

Minimum commitments appear in:
- **Minimum purchase obligations** - Required minimum quantities over specified periods
- **Minimum guarantee payments** - Required minimum payments regardless of actual purchases
- **Minimum sales or distribution requirements** - Required minimum sales efforts or volumes
- **Minimum advertising or marketing spend** - Required minimum promotional expenditures
- **Minimum number of sales representatives** - Required staffing levels
- **Minimum details or calls** - Required minimum sales calls or presentations
- **Take-or-pay provisions** - Must pay even if minimum not taken

#### Key Contractual Patterns Observed

**Pattern 1: Minimum purchase quantities**
- *Example from Sekisui/Qualigen agreement:* "as to Instruments the quantities for each of the first three months of each respective Forecast shall be deemed to constitute and shall constitute firm, binding orders"
- *Example from iShip/MBE agreement:* Exclusivity termination if "MBE and all MBE Centers together fail to ship at least [***] packages manifested by the Service in any period of three full months"

**Pattern 2: Minimum sales force or activity levels**
- *Example from Dova/Valeant agreement:* "Valeant shall maintain at least one hundred (100) Sales Representatives with responsibility to Detail the Product"
- *Example from Dova/Valeant agreement:* "Quarterly Minimum Details" defined as [***]

**Pattern 3: Minimum marketing spend**
- *Example from MusclePharm agreement:* "MusclePharm shall allocate between $3,000,000 and $5,000,000 in the First Contract Year... toward the marketing of the Licensed Products"

**Pattern 4: Minimum guarantee payments**
- *Example from Glu Mobile/Fox agreement:* "Licensee shall pay to Fox a non-refundable guarantee in the amount of US$[***] ('Guarantee')" - This is a minimum payment regardless of actual sales

**Pattern 5: Minimum development obligations**
- *Example from Glu Mobile/Fox agreement:* "Licensee acknowledges that it shall be obligated to develop all Wireless Products set forth on Exhibits B, C, D, and G"

**Pattern 6: Minimum performance thresholds for renewal**
- *Example from MusclePharm agreement:* "In the event that MusclePharm shall achieve Net Sales of $20 million... then this Agreement shall automatically be renewed"

#### Review Steps

1. **Search for "minimum," "guarantee," "obligation," "commitment," "shall"** - These terms often introduce minimum requirements
2. **Check purchase order provisions** - Are forecasts binding for certain periods?
3. **Review exclusivity provisions** - Exclusivity is often conditioned on minimum performance
4. **Examine termination clauses** - Can the agreement be terminated for failure to meet minimums?
5. **Look for take-or-pay provisions** - Must payments be made even if minimum quantities aren't taken?
6. **Check renewal conditions** - Are there minimum thresholds for automatic renewal?

### Category 4: Volume Restriction

**Question:** Is there a fee increase or consent requirement, etc. if one party's use of the product/services exceeds certain threshold?

**Answer Format:** Yes/No

#### What to Look For

Volume restrictions appear in:
- **Tiered pricing** - Different prices or fees at different volume levels
- **Consent requirements for expansion** - Need approval to exceed certain volumes
- **Fee increases at thresholds** - Additional fees triggered by volume
- **Capacity limitations** - Maximum volumes that can be supplied or used
- **Overage charges** - Additional costs for exceeding limits
- **Bundling restrictions** - Limits on combining with other products/services
- **Use restrictions** - Limits on how much can be used or distributed

#### Key Contractual Patterns Observed

**Pattern 1: Tiered pricing based on volume thresholds**
- *Example from iShip/MBE agreement:* Bounty Fee decreases as volume increases - "[***] per Bounty Package" at lower volumes, decreasing to "[***]" at higher volumes
- *Example from Glu Mobile/Fox agreement:* Royalty rates change at recoupment thresholds - "[***]% until [***]% of Individual Property Guarantee is recouped, then [***]%"

**Pattern 2: Consent requirements for expansion**
- *Example from MusclePharm agreement:* "there shall initially be no less than four (4) Products at the start of the Term and thereafter no more than 8 (eight) Products in the AS Product Line without the mutual written agreement of the parties"
- *Example from Sekisui/Qualigen agreement:* Additional wells require "mutually agreed" locations and regulatory approvals

**Pattern 3: Volume-based exclusivity termination**
- *Example from iShip/MBE agreement:* "Company may... terminate the exclusivity provisions... if MBE and all MBE Centers together fail to ship at least [***] packages manifested by the Service in any period of three full months"

**Pattern 4: Capacity or supply limitations**
- *Example from STW Resources/COFS agreement:* "availability of a minimum 1,200 gallons per minute... CRA water production" - This sets a minimum, but also implies maximum capacity constraints

**Pattern 5: Threshold-based revenue sharing changes**
- *Example from Kubient/AP agreement:* Revenue share changes from 90%/10% to 50%/50% once monthly revenue exceeds $300,000 for Programmatic/Display

#### Review Steps

1. **Examine pricing schedules** - Look for tiered pricing or volume-based fee changes
2. **Check for "threshold," "exceed," "over," "additional"** - These terms often indicate volume restrictions
3. **Review exclusivity provisions** - Exclusivity may be conditioned on volume performance
4. **Look for consent requirements** - Does expansion beyond certain levels require approval?
5. **Check capacity or supply provisions** - Are there limits on how much can be supplied?
6. **Examine use restrictions** - Are there limits on how the product/service can be used?
