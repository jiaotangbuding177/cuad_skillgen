# SKILL.md: Intellectual Property & License Review

## Overview

This skill enables systematic review of contracts to identify and analyze intellectual property (IP) and licensing provisions. The reviewer examines contracts for license grants, transfer restrictions, affiliate rights, usage limitations, perpetuity clauses, source code escrow arrangements, and post-termination obligations. This skill is essential for due diligence, risk assessment, and understanding the scope of IP rights transferred or retained under an agreement.

The review covers eight specific categories, each requiring identification of particular contractual language and understanding of how IP rights are structured between parties.

---

## Review Steps

### Step 1: Identify the Contract Type and Parties
- Determine the nature of the agreement (e.g., franchise, supply, collaboration, joint venture, trademark license, development agreement)
- Identify which party is the licensor/grantor and which is the licensee/grantee
- Note any affiliate definitions and whether affiliates are included as parties

### Step 2: Scan for License Grant Language
- Look for sections titled "License Grant," "License," "Intellectual Property," or similar
- Identify keywords: "grants," "licenses," "right to use," "authorizes," "permission"
- Determine if the license is express or implied

### Step 3: Examine License Scope and Restrictions
- Check for field-of-use restrictions, territorial limitations, and exclusivity provisions
- Look for transferability language: "non-transferable," "non-assignable," "personal"
- Identify affiliate-related provisions: "Affiliate," "subsidiary," "related company"

### Step 4: Assess Duration and Revocability
- Look for "perpetual," "irrevocable," "term," "duration" language
- Check termination provisions for conditions that could revoke the license
- Identify any "survival" clauses that extend license rights beyond termination

### Step 5: Identify Special Provisions
- Search for "escrow," "source code," "bankruptcy," "insolvency" for source code escrow
- Look for "post-termination," "transition," "wind-down," "last buy," "transfer of IP"
- Check for "enterprise," "all you can eat," "unlimited" usage language

### Step 6: Document Findings
- Record answers for each category with supporting citations
- Note any ambiguities or missing provisions
- Flag provisions that require further legal analysis

## Category-Specific Review Guidance

### 1. License Grant (Yes/No)

**What to Look For:**
- Express language granting permission to use IP: "grants," "licenses," "right and license"
- Implied grants through authorization language: "authorized to," "permitted to"
- Even limited or restricted grants count as "Yes"

**Examples from Contracts:**
- *Trademark License Agreement*: "Licensor hereby grants to Licensee... a personal, non-exclusive, royalty-free right and license to use the Licensed Mark"
- *Franchise Agreement*: "We hereby grant you a license to use the 'Buffalo Wild Wings' Marks and System"
- *Cooperation Agreement*: "COFS hereby grants, leases and lets unto STW the right to explore for, drill for, produce, utilize, transport, and treat groundwater"

**Answer "No" only if:**
- No IP rights are being licensed (e.g., pure services agreement)
- The agreement explicitly states no license is granted
- Only tangible property rights are conveyed

---

### 2. Non-Transferable License (Yes/No)

**What to Look For:**
- Explicit "non-transferable," "non-assignable," or "personal" language
- Restrictions on assignment or sublicensing
- Consent requirements for transfer that effectively limit transferability

**Examples from Contracts:**
- *Trademark License Agreement*: "a personal, non-exclusive, royalty-free right and license" (the word "personal" indicates non-transferable)
- *Franchise Agreement*: "You shall not relocate your Franchised Restaurant without our prior written approval"
- *Supply Agreement*: "Licensee shall not sublicense, assign, pledge... without the prior written consent of Licensor"

**Answer "No" only if:**
- The license is expressly transferable or assignable
- The agreement explicitly permits assignment without restriction
- The license runs with the business or is transferable upon change of control

---

### 3. Affiliate License - Licensor (Yes/No)

**What to Look For:**
- License grants that include IP owned by affiliates of the licensor
- Language such as "licensor and its affiliates," "on behalf of itself and its affiliates"
- Definitions of "Licensor Group" or similar that include affiliates

**Examples from Contracts:**
- *Intellectual Property Agreement*: "a Licensor Party, on behalf of itself and the other members of the Licensor Group... hereby grants... a royalty-free, nonexclusive, perpetual, irrevocable... license"
- *Development Agreement*: "Stryker Background IP" defined to include IP owned by Stryker and its Affiliates
- *Collaboration Agreement*: "BLI Background IP" defined as IP "Controlled by BLI or its Affiliates"

**Answer "No" if:**
- Only the named party's IP is licensed
- Affiliates are explicitly excluded from the license grant
- The agreement defines IP as only that owned by the specific legal entity

---

### 4. Affiliate License - Licensee (Yes/No)

**What to Look For:**
- License grants that extend to affiliates of the licensee
- Language such as "to Licensee and its Affiliates," "Licensee Group"
- Rights for affiliates to use, sublicense, or benefit from the license

**Examples from Contracts:**
- *Intellectual Property Agreement*: "grants... to the applicable Licensee Party and the other members of the Licensee Group"
- *Development Agreement*: "Joint IP may be used freely by either Party or its Affiliates"
- *Franchise Agreement*: Typically does NOT extend to franchisee affiliates (franchise is personal)

**Answer "No" if:**
- The license is granted only to the named licensee entity
- Affiliates are not mentioned or are explicitly excluded
- The license is "personal" to the licensee

---

### 5. Unlimited/All-You-Can-Eat License (Yes/No)

**What to Look For:**
- Terms like "enterprise-wide," "unlimited," "all you can eat," "unrestricted"
- No quantity, volume, or usage caps on the license
- Royalty-free or flat-fee pricing without per-use charges

**Examples from Contracts:**
- *Intellectual Property Agreement*: "royalty-free, nonexclusive, perpetual, irrevocable, fully paid-up, worldwide right and license" (the "fully paid-up" and "worldwide" nature suggests unlimited scope within the field)
- *Trademark License Agreement*: "royalty-free right and license" without usage restrictions

**Answer "No" if:**
- There are quantity limits, volume caps, or per-unit fees
- The license is limited to specific products, territories, or fields of use
- Usage is tied to specific metrics or measurements

---

### 6. Irrevocable or Perpetual License (Yes/No)

**What to Look For:**
- Explicit "irrevocable" or "perpetual" language
- License that survives termination of the agreement
- "Perpetual" term or "in perpetuity" language

**Examples from Contracts:**
- *Intellectual Property Agreement*: "perpetual, irrevocable... right and license" and "The provisions of this Agreement, including the license rights... shall not be terminable or revocable for any reason"
- *Trademark License Agreement*: "The license granted... shall continue perpetually"
- *Franchise Agreement*: Typically NOT perpetual (has fixed term with renewal options)

**Answer "No" if:**
- The license has a definite term
- The license terminates with the agreement
- The license is revocable for any reason (e.g., "at will" termination)

---

### 7. Source Code Escrow (Yes/No)

**What to Look For:**
- "Escrow," "source code escrow," "deposit" language
- Conditions for release (bankruptcy, insolvency, breach)
- Third-party escrow agent provisions

**Examples from Contracts:**
- *Development Agreement*: Look for "Insolvency Event" definitions and escrow provisions (Note: The provided Conformis agreement defines Insolvency Event but does not appear to contain a source code escrow requirement)
- *Supply Agreement*: Typically does not involve source code escrow

**Answer "No" if:**
- No mention of source code deposit or escrow
- The agreement does not involve software or source code
- Only object code or executable code is provided

---

### 8. Post-Termination Services (Yes/No)

**What to Look For:**
- Obligations that survive termination or expiration
- Transition services, wind-down periods, last-buy rights
- Transfer of IP, return of materials, or continued support obligations
- Non-compete or confidentiality that extends beyond termination

**Examples from Contracts:**
- *Franchise Agreement*: "Upon expiration or termination... you shall immediately... discontinue all other use of the Licensed Mark" and "For twenty-four (24) months following termination... Licensee shall specify... that Licensee is no longer operating under the Licensed Mark"
- *Supply Agreement*: "Upon the effective date of expiration or termination... Supplier shall deliver to CUTANEA all Products Manufactured hereunder under valid Purchase Orders"
- *Cooperation Agreement*: "Upon termination... STW... shall transfer title of all water conveyance pipelines... to COFS"
- *Development Agreement*: "The provisions of Articles V, VI, VII and IX... shall survive the termination or expiration of this Agreement"

**Answer "No" only if:**
- All obligations end immediately upon termination
- No transition, wind-down, or survival provisions exist
- The agreement explicitly states no post-termination obligations

## Key Patterns Observed in Contracts

1. **Trademark License Agreements** typically contain personal, non-transferable, royalty-free licenses with quality control provisions and termination upon change of control or cessation of advisory relationship.

2. **Franchise Agreements** grant limited, non-transferable licenses with strict territorial restrictions, ongoing fees, and extensive post-termination obligations including cessation of mark use.

3. **Development/Collaboration Agreements** often involve complex IP ownership structures with background IP, foreground IP, and joint IP provisions, plus cross-licenses for development purposes.

4. **Supply Agreements** may include limited IP licenses for manufacturing purposes but typically do not grant broad usage rights.

5. **Corporate Separation/IP Agreements** (like the UTC/Otis/Carrier agreement) often contain broad, perpetual, irrevocable cross-licenses with field restrictions and extensive survival provisions.

6. **Joint Venture Agreements** may not contain express IP licenses but instead address IP ownership through capital contribution and profit-sharing provisions.

7. **Source code escrow** is uncommon in the provided contracts but should be checked in any software-related agreement.

8. **Post-termination services** are most commonly found in franchise agreements (mark cessation, non-compete), supply agreements (last-buy, transition), and complex IP agreements (survival of licenses, return of materials).
