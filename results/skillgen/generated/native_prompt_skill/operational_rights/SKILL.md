# SKILL.md - Contract Review: Operational Rights

## Overview

This skill enables systematic review of contracts to identify and extract specific operational rights provisions. The review focuses on five key categories: Rights of First Refusal/Offer/Negotiation (ROFR/ROFO/ROFN), Intellectual Property Ownership Assignment, Joint IP Ownership, Covenants Not to Sue, and Third Party Beneficiary rights.

This skill is essential for due diligence, risk assessment, and understanding the operational and legal constraints embedded in commercial agreements.

## Review Steps

### Step 1: Initial Contract Scan
- Read the contract title, parties, and recitals to understand the nature of the agreement
- Identify the type of contract (license, joint venture, franchise, distribution, marketing, servicing, etc.)
- Note the governing law and jurisdiction clauses

### Step 2: Systematic Category Review
For each of the five categories, follow the specific steps below.

---

## Category 1: ROFR/ROFO/ROFN

### What to Look For
Search for clauses granting one party a right of first refusal, right of first offer, or right of first negotiation to purchase, license, market, or distribute equity interest, technology, assets, products, or services.

### Key Search Terms
- "right of first refusal"
- "right of first offer"
- "right of first negotiation"
- "ROFR"
- "first opportunity"
- "first right"
- "option to purchase"

### Patterns Observed in Contracts

**Pattern 1: ROFR for Expansion of Territory or Scope**
Found in marketing/distribution agreements. Example from XpresspaGroupInc Marketing Agreement:
> "Calm shall have a right of first refusal to expand the rights and obligations described in this Agreement to any Stores outside the Territory (the 'ROFR'). XSPA shall give prompt written notice to Calm each time it offers... Calm shall have thirty (30) business days... to exercise its ROFR."

**Pattern 2: ROFR for Purchase of Company or Assets**
Found in distributor/license agreements. Example from NEONSystems Distributor Agreement Amendment:
> "If, at any time or from time to time during the term hereof, Licensor or any stockholder in Licensor shall have received a bona fide offer from any person or entity to sell, transfer or otherwise convey all or any stock in, or assets of, Licensor... the Offeree shall first give written notice... to Licensee of the financial and other terms... Licensee shall have the right and a first opportunity to purchase."

**Pattern 3: Option to Purchase (Related but Distinct)**
Sometimes found alongside ROFR. Example from NEONSystems:
> "Skunkware and Licensor hereby grant to Licensee the exclusive and irrevocable right and option to purchase... either (i) all of the assets of Licensor or (ii) all of the issued and outstanding stock of Licensor."

### Decision Rules
- **Answer "Yes"** if the contract contains any clause granting a right of first refusal, first offer, or first negotiation for any of the specified items (equity, technology, assets, products, services)
- **Answer "No"** if no such clause exists
- Note: Options to purchase that are not conditional on a third-party offer are NOT ROFR/ROFO/ROFN

## Category 2: IP Ownership Assignment

### What to Look For
Clauses where intellectual property created by one party becomes the property of the counterparty, either automatically or upon occurrence of certain events.

### Key Search Terms
- "assigns all right, title and interest"
- "shall be owned by"
- "hereby assigns"
- "work made for hire"
- "shall vest in"
- "shall become the sole and exclusive property of"

### Patterns Observed in Contracts

**Pattern 1: Automatic Assignment of Inventions**
Found in license and collaboration agreements. Example from CytodynInc License Agreement:
> "All right, title and interest in and to any and all Inventions... will be owned by CytoDyn. To the fullest extent permitted by law, Vyera shall, and hereby does, assign all of its right title and interest in and to any and all Inventions to CytoDyn."

**Pattern 2: Assignment of Product Collateral IP**
Found in marketing agreements. Example from XpresspaGroupInc:
> "Any intellectual property or moral right in any Product Collateral or other marketing materials concerning Calm or one or more Products... shall be, as between the Parties, the sole and exclusive property of Calm... If any Product Collateral IP... are not designed and/or created by Calm, such Product Collateral IP... shall be deemed 'works made for hire' for Calm... or... all ownership rights thereto shall be, and are hereby, assigned to Calm."

**Pattern 3: Assignment of Feedback**
Found in technology/hosting agreements. Example from Vitalibis Hosting Agreement:
> "Any Feedback submitted to VOTOCAST without Licensee providing such written notice, shall be deemed the sole property of VOTOCAST and the same, together with all copyrights, trade secrets, patent rights, and other intellectual property thereto shall be and hereby are assigned to VOTOCAST."

**Pattern 4: Assignment of Test Market Results**
Found in franchise agreements. Example from PfHospitalityGroup Franchise Agreement:
> "If, following the Test, we determine that we will approve the tested item, then for so long as we deem that item to be an 'approved item' under this Agreement, you will have the right to use that item... and we will have the right to use and market that item as we see fit... without compensation to you."

### Decision Rules
- **Answer "Yes"** if any clause provides that IP created by one party becomes property of the other party
- **Answer "No"** if IP ownership remains with the creating party or if there is no assignment provision
- Note: This includes both automatic assignment and assignment triggered by specific events

## Category 3: Joint IP Ownership

### What to Look For
Clauses providing for joint or shared ownership of intellectual property between the parties.

### Key Search Terms
- "jointly owned"
- "co-ownership"
- "joint ownership"
- "shared ownership"
- "owned jointly"
- "in equal shares"

### Patterns Observed in Contracts

**Pattern 1: Joint Ownership of Results**
Found in joint venture agreements. Example from KiromicBiopharma Joint Venture Agreement:
> "The Industrial Property Rights on the Results, as well as the Intellectual Property Rights realized in the research activities covered by this JV, are due jointly to the parties in equal shares (50% for each Party), without prejudice to the possibility of agreeing in writing... about the modification of the respective shares of co-ownership."

**Pattern 2: Joint Ownership with Separate Agreement for Management**
Same contract continues:
> "The parties will agree, by separate agreement, on the specific discipline relating to the management of rights in co-ownership."

**Pattern 3: Option to Convert to Joint Ownership**
Same contract:
> "If one of the Parties has no interest in applying for a patent, it will inform the other Party within 30 days... In this case the Party concerned shall have the right to proceed with the submission of the application on the Results at its own expense and in co-ownership with the other Party."

### Decision Rules
- **Answer "Yes"** if any clause provides for joint or shared ownership of IP between the parties
- **Answer "No"** if IP is owned solely by one party or if there is no joint ownership provision
- Note: This includes both equal and unequal sharing arrangements

## Category 4: Covenant Not to Sue

### What to Look For
Clauses restricting a party from contesting the validity of the counterparty's IP ownership or from bringing claims against the counterparty for matters unrelated to the contract.

### Key Search Terms
- "covenant not to sue"
- "shall not challenge"
- "shall not contest"
- "shall not bring any claim"
- "shall not assert"
- "acknowledges and agrees that... is the sole and exclusive owner"
- "shall not do or cause to be done any act or thing that may in any way adversely affect any rights"

### Patterns Observed in Contracts

**Pattern 1: Covenant Not to Challenge IP Ownership**
Found in marketing/license agreements. Example from XpresspaGroupInc:
> "Each Party acknowledges and agrees that, as between the Parties, the other Party is the sole and exclusive owner of the Marks licensed by such other Party... and all goodwill associated therewith. Neither Party shall do or cause to be done any act or thing that may in any way adversely affect any rights of the other Party in and to such other Party's Marks or any registrations thereof... including challenging the ownership, validity or enforceability of such Marks."

**Pattern 2: Covenant Not to Compete (Related but Distinct)**
Found in license agreements. Example from CytodynInc:
> "Vyera hereby covenants not to Develop, Manufacture, Commercialize or otherwise exploit a Competitive Product in the Territory during the Royalty Term."

**Pattern 3: Covenant Regarding Non-Infringement Representations**
Found in hosting agreements. Example from Vitalibis:
> "Licensee warrants... that VOTOCAST'S use of any data, information, or materials... provided by Licensee or Licensee Users does not and will not... infringe on or violate any applicable law, regulation or right of a third party."

### Decision Rules
- **Answer "Yes"** if any clause restricts a party from contesting IP validity or bringing claims unrelated to the contract
- **Answer "No"** if no such restriction exists
- Note: Standard non-infringement representations and warranties are NOT covenants not to sue
- Note: Non-compete clauses are NOT covenants not to sue unless they specifically address IP challenges

## Category 5: Third Party Beneficiary

### What to Look For
Clauses indicating that a non-contracting party is a beneficiary to some or all of the contract's clauses and can enforce its rights against a contracting party.

### Key Search Terms
- "third party beneficiary"
- "third-party beneficiary"
- "shall be deemed a third-party beneficiary"
- "may enforce"
- "for the benefit of"
- "intended beneficiary"

### Patterns Observed in Contracts

**Pattern 1: Explicit Third Party Beneficiary Designation**
Found in servicing agreements. Example from CUROGroupHoldings Servicing Agreement:
> "Notwithstanding anything to the contrary in this Agreement, both the Owner and Servicer agree that the Agent shall be deemed to be a third-party beneficiary of this Agreement and has the authority to enforce the provisions hereof."

**Pattern 2: No Third Party Beneficiaries (Negative Statement)**
Found in marketing agreements. Example from Loop_Industries Marketing Agreement:
> "This Assignment is solely for the benefit of the Assignor and the Assignee and their successors and permitted assigns, and no right or cause of action shall accrue by reason hereof for the benefit of any third party."

**Pattern 3: Indemnification for Third Party Claims (Not Beneficiary)**
Common in many contracts - these are indemnification obligations, not third party beneficiary rights.

### Decision Rules
- **Answer "Yes"** if any clause explicitly states that a non-contracting party is a third party beneficiary with enforcement rights
- **Answer "No"** if there is no such clause, or if the contract explicitly states there are no third party beneficiaries
- Note: Indemnification clauses that protect against third party claims do NOT create third party beneficiaries
- Note: The mere mention of "third parties" does not create third party beneficiary status
