## Common Clause Patterns & Example Phrasing

### 1. Rofr/Rofo/Rofn (Right of First Refusal/Offer/Negotiation)

**Pattern 1: Explicit Grant of Right of First Refusal for Specific Opportunities**
*   **Description:** The contract grants one party the right to match or participate in specific business opportunities (e.g., promotions, licensing) before the other party engages third parties.
*   **Example Phrasing:**
    > "During the Term, Women.com agrees to give eDiets the right of first refusal to participate in all diet and diet-counseling related promotion opportunities created or otherwise made available by Women..."
    *   **Evidence:** [KA-0218]
*   **Variation Notes:** Rights may be limited to specific categories (e.g., "diet-counseling") or specific types of works (e.g., "theatrical motion pictures"). See [KA-0307] for Right of First Negotiation variations.

**Pattern 2: Explicit Grant of Right of First Offer for New Works**
*   **Description:** One party must offer new intellectual property or works to the other party before creating them or licensing them to third parties.
*   **Example Phrasing:**
    > "PFHOF agrees that during the Term, except with respect to the categories identified on Exhibit A, it will not create new PFHOF Works without first granting the Village Media Company a right of first o..."
    *   **Evidence:** [KA-0318]
*   **Variation Notes:** May include exceptions for specific categories listed in exhibits. See [KA-0317] for Right of First Refusal on licensing new works.

**Pattern 3: Absence of ROFR/ROFO/ROFN Clauses**
*   **Description:** The contract explicitly states or implies that no rights of first refusal, offer, or negotiation exist. This is often confirmed by the absence of such language in the entire agreement.
*   **Example Phrasing:**
    > "No clause granting a right of first refusal, right of first offer, or right of first negotiation was found in the contract."
    *   **Evidence:** [KA-0174], [KA-0001], [KA-0014]
*   **Variation Notes:** In many standard agreements (e.g., Reseller, Outsourcing), these rights are simply not included. See [KA-0085], [KA-0090].

### 2. IP Ownership Assignment

**Pattern 1: Work-for-Hire and Assignment of Deliverables**
*   **Description:** The contract designates created materials as "works for hire" and explicitly assigns all rights, title, and interest to the client/company.
*   **Example Phrasing:**
    > "The Developer expressly acknowledges and agrees that any all proprietary materials prepared by the Developer under this Agreement shall be considered "works for hire" and the exclusive property of the..."
    *   **Evidence:** [KA-0144]
*   **Variation Notes:** Often accompanied by a fallback assignment clause if work-for-hire status is not recognized by law. See [KA-0145].

**Pattern 2: Assignment of Modifications and Derivative Works**
*   **Description:** Ownership of improvements, modifications, or derivative works created by either party is assigned to a specific party (often the licensor or the party paying for the development).
*   **Example Phrasing:**
    > "All changes, modifications and enhancements or derivative works made to the Software or Documentation by Corio or Changepoint, or jointly by the parties, shall be owned by Changepoint, including all c..."
    *   **Evidence:** [KA-0099]
*   **Variation Notes:** May distinguish between background IP (retained by creator) and foreground IP (assigned). See [KA-0126], [KA-0160].

**Pattern 3: Retention of Background IP / No Assignment**
*   **Description:** The contract explicitly states that each party retains ownership of its pre-existing IP and that no ownership is transferred for specific content.
*   **Example Phrasing:**
    > "Nothing in this Agreement shall effect a transfer of ownership in the eDiets Content from eDiets to Women.com, and eDiets shall retain all rights of copyright, trademark or other intellectual property..."
    *   **Evidence:** [KA-0219]
*   **Variation Notes:** Common in licensing agreements where only usage rights are granted. See [KA-0002], [KA-0140].

### 3. Joint IP Ownership

**Pattern 1: Explicit Joint Ownership of Jointly Created IP**
*   **Description:** The contract establishes that IP created jointly by employees or agents of both parties is owned jointly, often specifying equal shares.
*   **Example Phrasing:**
    > "The Parties will jointly own all inventions and other Intellectual Property Rights jointly made under this Agreement that are directly resulting from work conducted under this Agreement in accordance ..."
    *   **Evidence:** [KA-0087]
*   **Variation Notes:** May specify the percentage of ownership (e.g., 50/50). See [KA-0428].

**Pattern 2: Assignment of Undivided Interest to Formalize Joint Ownership**
*   **Description:** To ensure legal clarity, parties agree to assign an undivided interest in jointly created IP to the other party.
*   **Example Phrasing:**
    > "Each Party to whom ownership is to vest in Joint IP by operation of law or by assignment by its employees or Agents agrees to assign and hereby assigns to the other Party an undivided one-half right, ..."
    *   **Evidence:** [KA-0162]
*   **Variation Notes:** Often paired with definitions of "Joint IP" vs. "Background IP". See [KA-0161].

**Pattern 3: Absence of Joint Ownership Provisions**
*   **Description:** The contract explicitly states that no joint ownership exists, or that all IP is solely owned by one party.
*   **Example Phrasing:**
    > "All of the Transferred Patents are owned solely by SONY. No Transferred Patent is jointly owned by SONY and any other Person, nor is it owned or jointly owned by any SONY Affiliate."
    *   **Evidence:** [KA-0293]
*   **Variation Notes:** Common in consulting or manufacturing agreements where the client retains sole ownership. See [KA-0003], [KA-0116].

### 4. Covenant Not to Sue

**Pattern 1: Restriction on Challenging IP Validity**
*   **Description:** One party agrees not to contest the validity, ownership, or enforceability of the other party's intellectual property rights.
*   **Example Phrasing:**
    > "Women.com acknowledges that eDiets owns all right, title and interest in and to the eDiets Content, and Women.com shall not now or in the future contest the validity of the eDiets' ownership rights in..."
    *   **Evidence:** [KA-0222]
*   **Variation Notes:** May apply to trademarks, copyrights, or patents. See [KA-0363] for trademark-specific covenants.

**Pattern 2: Mutual Non-Challenge of Marks**
*   **Description:** Both parties agree not to attack, challenge, or file applications against the other party's trademarks or marks.
*   **Example Phrasing:**
    > "At no time during the term of the Agreement or thereafter shall eDiets attack, challenge or file any application with respect to any Women.com Mark. At no time during the term of the Agreement or ther..."
    *   **Evidence:** [KA-0223]
*   **Variation Notes:** Often found in co-branding or partnership agreements. See [KA-0356], [KA-0357].

**Pattern 3: Absence of Covenant Not to Sue**
*   **Description:** The contract does not contain any clauses restricting a party from contesting the validity of the counterparty's IP.
*   **Example Phrasing:**
    > "No clause restricting a party from contesting the validity of the counterparty’s ownership of intellectual property or bringing unrelated claims was found in the contract."
    *   **Evidence:** [KA-0177]
*   **Variation Notes:** Common in standard service agreements where IP validity is not a central issue. See [KA-0004], [KA-0017].

### 5. Third Party Beneficiary

**Pattern 1: Explicit Exclusion of Third Party Beneficiaries**
*   **Description:** The contract states that it is solely for the benefit of the contracting parties and their successors/assigns, excluding any third-party rights.
*   **Example Phrasing:**
    > "This Agreement is solely for the benefit of the parties hereto and is not binding upon or enforceable by any other persons."
    *   **Evidence:** [KA-0013]
*   **Variation Notes:** Standard boilerplate in most commercial contracts. See [KA-0025], [KA-0193].

**Pattern 2: Designation of Specific Third Party Beneficiary**
*   **Description:** The contract explicitly identifies a non-contracting party (e.g., an Agent, Affiliate, or Group Member) as a beneficiary with enforcement rights.
*   **Example Phrasing:**
    > "Notwithstanding anything to the contrary in this Agreement, both the Owner and Servicer agree that the Agent shall be deemed to be a third-party beneficiary of this Agreement and has the authority to ..."
    *   **Evidence:** [KA-0125]
*   **Variation Notes:** May be limited to specific provisions (e.g., indemnification) or specific events (e.g., transfer of license). See [KA-0377], [KA-0378].

**Pattern 3: Absence of Third Party Beneficiary Clauses**
*   **Description:** The contract does not explicitly address third-party beneficiaries, implying no such rights exist unless otherwise stated by law.
*   **Example Phrasing:**
    > "No clause designating a third-party beneficiary or excluding third-party rights was found in the contract."
    *   **Evidence:** [KA-0005], [KA-0036], [KA-0118]
*   **Variation Notes:** In the absence of an exclusion clause, courts may interpret intent differently, but the evidence index treats "No" as no explicit designation.

## Review Checklist

### Rofr/Rofo/Rofn
- [ ] **Check for Explicit Grants:** Search for "right of first refusal," "right of first offer," or "right of first negotiation." If found, verify the scope (e.g., specific products, promotions). [KA-0218, KA-0307, KA-0318]
- [ ] **Check for Exceptions:** If a right is granted, check for exclusions (e.g., "except for categories in Exhibit A"). [KA-0317, KA-0318]
- [ ] **Confirm Absence:** If no such clauses are found, confirm that the contract does not implicitly grant these rights. [KA-0174, KA-0001]

### IP Ownership Assignment
- [ ] **Identify Work-for-Hire Clauses:** Look for "works for hire" and "assigns all right, title, and interest." [KA-0144, KA-0145]
- [ ] **Check for Modification Ownership:** Determine who owns improvements, derivatives, or customizations. [KA-0099, KA-0126]
- [ ] **Verify Background IP Retention:** Ensure that pre-existing IP is explicitly retained by the original owner. [KA-0219, KA-0002]

### Joint IP Ownership
- [ ] **Search for Joint Ownership Language:** Look for "jointly own," "undivided interest," or "shared ownership." [KA-0087, KA-0162]
- [ ] **Define Joint IP:** Check if the contract defines what constitutes "Joint IP" vs. "Background IP." [KA-0161]
- [ ] **Confirm Sole Ownership:** If no joint ownership is stated, verify that sole ownership is assigned to one party. [KA-0293, KA-0003]

### Covenant Not to Sue
- [ ] **Check for Non-Challenge Clauses:** Search for "shall not contest," "shall not challenge," or "covenant not to sue." [KA-0222, KA-0223]
- [ ] **Identify Scope:** Determine if the covenant applies to all IP or specific marks/patents. [KA-0363, KA-0356]
- [ ] **Confirm Absence:** If no such clause exists, note that parties may retain the right to challenge IP validity. [KA-0177, KA-0004]

### Third Party Beneficiary
- [ ] **Check for Exclusion Clauses:** Look for "solely for the benefit of the parties," "no third-party beneficiaries." [KA-0013, KA-0025]
- [ ] **Identify Specific Beneficiaries:** If present, identify who is designated as a beneficiary (e.g., Agent, Affiliate). [KA-0125, KA-0377]
- [ ] **Confirm Absence:** If no clause is present, note that third-party rights are not explicitly addressed. [KA-0005, KA-0036]
