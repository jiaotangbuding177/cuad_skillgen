# SKILL.md: Operational Rights & IP Governance Review

## Covered Categories

This skill analyzes contracts for specific operational rights, intellectual property (IP) ownership structures, and third-party enforcement rights.

1.  **Rofr/Rofo/Rofn** (Right of First Refusal/Offer/Negotiation)
    *   **Discovery Frequency:** ~12% of contracts (36/306).
    *   **Common Patterns:** Clauses granting preemptive rights to purchase, license, or negotiate for new products, services, or equity interests before third parties are approached. Often tied to specific timeframes (e.g., 30-60 days) and "good faith" negotiation requirements.

2.  **IP Ownership Assignment**
    *   **Discovery Frequency:** ~30% of contracts (91/306).
    *   **Common Patterns:** "Work-for-hire" provisions where the hiring party owns all created IP; explicit assignment clauses transferring rights upon creation; separation/spin-off mandates requiring execution of assignment documents; license agreements stating licensee acquires no ownership interest.

3.  **Joint IP Ownership**
    *   **Discovery Frequency:** ~7% of contracts (21/306).
    *   **Common Patterns:** Shared ownership of IP created through joint inventorship or cooperation; equitable sharing of royalties/benefits; specific definitions for "Joint Intellectual Property" in strategic alliances or co-branding deals.

4.  **Covenant Not to Sue**
    *   **Discovery Frequency:** ~10% of contracts (30/306).
    *   **Common Patterns:** Prohibitions on challenging the validity, ownership, or enforceability of the counterparty’s IP (trademarks, patents, domain names); mutual covenants in spin-offs not to sue for pre-existing IP infringement; restrictions on assisting third parties in such challenges.

5.  **Third Party Beneficiary**
    *   **Discovery Frequency:** ~18% of contracts (54/306).
    *   **Common Patterns:** Explicit statements that there are **no** third-party beneficiaries (limiting enforcement to signing parties); exceptions granting enforcement rights to affiliates, officers, directors, or specific customers (e.g., in indemnification or insurance clauses).

## Common Patterns

### Rofr/Rofo/Rofn
*   **Time-Bound Exercise:** Rights are frequently exercisable within a specific window (e.g., "within thirty days of notice").
    *   *Example:* "Ceres exercises this option within thirty days of IGER's notice."
*   **Scope-Specific Triggers:** Rights apply to specific business activities like new product development, territory expansion, or manufacturing requests.
    *   *Example:* "Right of first negotiation to add new products developed by Energous... before Energous can market them to third parties."
*   **Good Faith Requirement:** ROFN clauses often mandate negotiations in good faith before the right can be declined or exercised.

### IP Ownership Assignment
*   **Work-for-Hire Default:** Standard in services/consulting agreements where all work product is deemed the exclusive property of the client.
    *   *Example:* "All innovations created by the Consultant... are works made for hire and the sole exclusive property of the Company."
*   **No Interest in License:** License agreements explicitly state the licensee gains no ownership, and goodwill inures to the licensor.
    *   *Example:* "Telnet acquires no interest in Galaxy's trademarks... Galaxy retains ownership of all records."
*   **Mandatory Assignment Execution:** In spin-offs or collaborations, parties must execute specific documents to formalize IP transfer.
    *   *Example:* "Parties to execute assignment agreements to record the transfer of intellectual property rights... as allocated in the Separation Agreement."

### Joint IP Ownership
*   **Joint Inventorship Trigger:** Ownership is shared only if both parties contribute to the invention/creation.
    *   *Example:* "Inventions invented jointly by employees or agents of both Theravance and GSK shall be owned jointly."
*   **Undivided Interest:** Joint IP is often held as an undivided interest, with joint assignment of patent applications.
    *   *Example:* "Ceres and IGER shall have an undivided interest in Joint Intellectual Property... applications... assigned jointly."
*   **Data Sharing:** Joint ownership often extends to specific data sets (e.g., user data, transactional data) in platform/co-branding agreements.

### Covenant Not to Sue
*   **Validity Challenge Prohibition:** Licensees are barred from contesting the validity of the licensor’s IP.
    *   *Example:* "Licensee agrees not to challenge or contest the Licensor's exclusive ownership rights of the Licensed Mark."
*   **Mutual Non-Disparagement of IP:** In separation agreements, both parties agree not to challenge each other’s IP registrations.
    *   *Example:* "SpinCo covenants not to oppose... any application... by Nuance... and Nuance covenants not to oppose... by SpinCo."
*   **Third-Party Assistance Ban:** Prohibition on helping others challenge the IP.
    *   *Example:* "Turpin covenants that he will not... assist any third party in contesting the Company's right... in the Work Product."

### Third Party Beneficiary
*   **Explicit Exclusion:** Most common pattern is a clause stating no third parties have rights.
    *   *Example:* "Contract... does not confer rights on any third persons."
*   **Indemnification Exceptions:** Specific groups (officers, directors, affiliates) are granted beneficiary status for indemnification purposes.
    *   *Example:* "Indemnification clauses that extend to officers, directors, agents... of both the Company and the Agent."
*   **Customer/End-User Rights:** Specific customers may be named as beneficiaries for warranty or insurance enforcement.
    *   *Example:* "Mediacom, LLC (the Customer)... included in the indemnification clause, granting them enforceable rights."

## Review Checklist

### Rofr/Rofo/Rofn
- [ ] Does the contract grant a Right of First Refusal (ROFR), Offer (ROFO), or Negotiation (ROFN)?
- [ ] What specific assets, products, or equity interests are covered?
- [ ] What is the timeframe for exercising the right (e.g., 30 days)?
- [ ] Are there conditions for exercise (e.g., good faith negotiation, matching terms)?

### IP Ownership Assignment
- [ ] Is there a "Work-for-Hire" clause assigning all created IP to the hiring party?
- [ ] Does the contract require the execution of separate assignment documents for IP transfer?
- [ ] Does the contract explicitly state that the licensee acquires **no** ownership interest in the licensor's IP?
- [ ] Are improvements or modifications created by one party assigned back to the other?

### Joint IP Ownership
- [ ] Is there a definition of "Joint Intellectual Property" or "Joint Inventions"?
- [ ] How is ownership shared (e.g., 50/50, undivided interest)?
- [ ] Are royalties or benefits from joint IP shared, and if so, how?
- [ ] Does joint ownership apply to specific data types (e.g., user data)?

### Covenant Not to Sue
- [ ] Is a party prohibited from challenging the validity, ownership, or enforceability of the other party’s IP?
- [ ] Does the covenant extend to trademarks, patents, copyrights, and domain names?
- [ ] Is there a prohibition on assisting third parties in challenging the IP?
- [ ] Are there exceptions (e.g., if sued for infringement)?

### Third Party Beneficiary
- [ ] Does the contract explicitly state there are **no** third-party beneficiaries?
- [ ] Are there exceptions for affiliates, officers, directors, or controlling persons?
- [ ] Are specific customers or end-users designated as beneficiaries with enforcement rights?
- [ ] Do indemnification or insurance clauses extend benefits to non-signing parties?

## Evidence Extraction Rules

1.  **Locate Keywords:** Search for terms like "Right of First Refusal," "Right of First Offer," "Right of First Negotiation," "Work for Hire," "Assignment," "Joint Ownership," "Covenant Not to Sue," "Challenge," "Validity," "Third Party Beneficiary," "No Third Party Rights."
2.  **Extract Context:**
    *   For **Rofr/Rofo/Rofn**: Extract the specific right granted, the subject matter (product/equity), the exercise period, and any conditions (good faith).
    *   For **IP Assignment**: Extract clauses stating ownership of work product, assignment obligations, or explicit denials of licensee ownership.
    *   For **Joint IP**: Extract definitions of joint IP, ownership percentages/shares, and royalty sharing mechanisms.
    *   For **Covenant Not to Sue**: Extract prohibitions on challenging IP validity, ownership, or enforceability, and any bans on assisting third parties.
    *   For **Third Party Beneficiary**: Extract clauses stating "no third-party beneficiaries" or explicitly naming beneficiaries (affiliates, customers, officers) and the scope of their rights (indemnification, enforcement).
3.  **Verify Scope:** Ensure the extracted clause applies to the relevant parties and assets defined in the case definition.

## Boundary Rules

*   **Do Not Infer Ownership:** If a contract is silent on IP ownership, do not assume work-for-hire or joint ownership. Mark as "not found" or "ambiguous" requiring human review if critical.
*   **Distinguish ROFR vs. ROFO:** Clearly differentiate between Right of First Refusal (reactive to a third-party offer) and Right of First Offer (proactive offer before third-party engagement).
*   **Exclude Standard Indemnities:** For Third Party Beneficiary analysis, only flag beneficiaries if they have explicit enforcement rights or are named as additional insureds/beneficiaries beyond standard indemnification language that merely protects them. However, if the indemnification clause explicitly names non-signing parties (officers, affiliates) as protected parties with rights, this counts as a third-party beneficiary exception.
*   **Covenant Scope:** Only flag "Covenant Not to Sue" if it specifically relates to IP validity/ownership challenges. General non-disparagement or non-compete clauses are out of scope.
*   **Joint IP Definition:** Ensure "Joint IP" is explicitly defined or clearly implied by joint inventorship clauses. Mere collaboration does not automatically imply joint ownership unless stated.
