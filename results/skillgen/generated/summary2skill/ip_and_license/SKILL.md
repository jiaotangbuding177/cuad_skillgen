# SKILL.md - IP and License Review Skill

## Covered Categories

### License Grant
- **Discovery frequency**: 31.7% of contracts (97/306)
- **Common patterns**: Non-exclusive, non-transferable, royalty-free licenses are most prevalent. Licenses are typically limited to specific purposes, fields, territories, or durations. Exclusive licenses appear in distribution and commercialization agreements.

### Non-Transferable License
- **Discovery frequency**: 28.4% of contracts (87/306)
- **Common patterns**: Transfer or assignment requires prior written consent. Exceptions exist for affiliates or mergers. Often coupled with non-sublicensability.

### Affiliate License-Licensor
- **Discovery frequency**: 15.0% of contracts (46/306)
- **Common patterns**: License includes IP owned or controlled by licensor's affiliates. Licensor remains responsible for affiliate performance. Licensor grants license on behalf of itself and its affiliates.

### Affiliate License-Licensee
- **Discovery frequency**: 15.0% of contracts (46/306)
- **Common patterns**: License granted to licensee and its affiliates. Licensee responsible for affiliate compliance. Affiliates permitted to use licensed IP under same terms.

### Unlimited/All-You-Can-Eat License
- **Discovery frequency**: 2.9% of contracts (9/306)
- **Common patterns**: Grants "unlimited" rights to use, reproduce, or sublicense. Often perpetual and irrevocable. May be limited by territory or term but not by users or copies.

### Irrevocable or Perpetual License
- **Discovery frequency**: 11.1% of contracts (34/306)
- **Common patterns**: License described as "perpetual" or "irrevocable". Often survives termination for certain purposes. Common for background IP or pre-existing work.

### Source Code Escrow
- **Discovery frequency**: 2.0% of contracts (6/306)
- **Common patterns**: Source code deposited with escrow agent. Release conditions include bankruptcy, breach, or dissolution. Common in software licensing and hosting agreements.

### Post-Termination Services
- **Discovery frequency**: 49.3% of contracts (151/306)
- **Common patterns**: Transition assistance or wind-down periods. Survival of confidentiality, indemnification, and limitation of liability. Return of property and materials.

## Common Patterns

### License Grant
1. **Non-exclusive, non-transferable, royalty-free license**: "Both parties grant each other non-exclusive rights to use their respective content and marks for the purposes of the agreement."
2. **Limited purpose license**: "Licensor grants Licensee a limited, non-exclusive, worldwide, royalty-free license to use the Licensed Mark for Licensed Products."
3. **Exclusive license for commercialization**: "Array grants Ono exclusive licenses under Array Patents to Develop, Manufacture, and Commercialize Products in the Ono Territory."

### Non-Transferable License
1. **Prior written consent required**: "Licensee may not assign, sublicense, or transfer its license rights without Licensor's prior written consent."
2. **Exception for mergers/acquisitions**: "The license granted is nontransferable except in accordance with Section 14.1, which allows assignment in connection with merger or acquisition."
3. **Non-transferable and non-sublicensable**: "All rights granted are not sublicenseable, transferable or assignable, except for certain limited exceptions."

### Affiliate License-Licensor
1. **License on behalf of affiliates**: "The license is granted by Licensor on behalf of itself and its Affiliates."
2. **IP controlled by affiliates**: "The license grant from BLI includes intellectual property controlled by BLI or its Affiliates."
3. **Licensor responsible for affiliates**: "Miltenyi may have Miltenyi Products manufactured by its Affiliates, and Miltenyi remains responsible for their performance."

### Affiliate License-Licensee
1. **License to licensee and affiliates**: "The license is granted to Licensee and its subsidiaries and affiliates."
2. **Sublicense to affiliates**: "The license grant to Ginkgo includes the right to sublicense to its Affiliates."
3. **Licensee responsible for affiliates**: "The license rights extend to Bank of America's Affiliates, and Bank of America is responsible for its Affiliates' obligations."

### Unlimited/All-You-Can-Eat License
1. **Unlimited usage rights**: "The grant is described as 'unlimited right and privilege' to use the Property."
2. **Unlimited sublicenses**: "Corio is granted an unlimited number of sublicenses to Customers and an unlimited number of internal users."
3. **Unlimited, perpetual, royalty-free**: "Individual grants Lifeway an unlimited, perpetual, non-exclusive, worldwide, royalty-free right to use her name, image, and likeness."

### Irrevocable or Perpetual License
1. **Perpetual and irrevocable**: "The license granted is perpetual and irrevocable."
2. **Perpetual for pre-existing work**: "The Client is granted a perpetual, irrevocable license to use pre-existing work incorporated into deliverables."
3. **Perpetual for end users**: "The contract grants a perpetual license for end users who previously downloaded a Zynga game that includes WPT's Licensed Property."

### Source Code Escrow
1. **Deposit with escrow agent**: "Commerce One agrees to deposit source code into escrow, which may be released to Corio upon dissolution or material breach."
2. **Release upon certain events**: "The contract requires M&I to deposit source code into escrow, which can be released to Customer upon certain events."
3. **Escrow agreement referenced**: "The contract references an Escrow Agreement as one of the Ancillary Agreements."

### Post-Termination Services
1. **Transition period**: "Upon termination, Licensee has a 90-day transition period to cease use of Licensed Domain Names and Content."
2. **Return/destruction of materials**: "Upon termination, the Distributor must cease using trademarks, return confidential information."
3. **Survival of obligations**: "Upon termination, i-Escrow must pay amounts owed, both parties must remove each other's content and marks, and certain provisions survive."

## Review Checklist

### License Grant
- [ ] Does the contract contain a license grant from one party to another?
- [ ] Is the license exclusive or non-exclusive?
- [ ] Is the license limited to specific purposes, fields, territories, or durations?
- [ ] Are there any royalty or payment obligations associated with the license?

### Non-Transferable License
- [ ] Does the license explicitly prohibit transfer or assignment?
- [ ] Are there exceptions for affiliates, mergers, or asset sales?
- [ ] Is prior written consent required for any transfer?
- [ ] Is the license also non-sublicensable?

### Affiliate License-Licensor
- [ ] Does the license include IP owned or controlled by the licensor's affiliates?
- [ ] Is the licensor responsible for its affiliates' performance?
- [ ] Does the licensor grant the license on behalf of itself and its affiliates?

### Affiliate License-Licensee
- [ ] Is the license granted to the licensee and its affiliates?
- [ ] Is the licensee responsible for its affiliates' compliance?
- [ ] Can the licensee sublicense to its affiliates without additional consent?

### Unlimited/All-You-Can-Eat License
- [ ] Does the license grant "unlimited" rights to use, reproduce, or sublicense?
- [ ] Is the license limited by territory or term but not by number of users or copies?
- [ ] Is the license perpetual and irrevocable?

### Irrevocable or Perpetual License
- [ ] Is the license described as "perpetual" or "irrevocable"?
- [ ] Does the license survive termination for certain purposes?
- [ ] Is the license granted for pre-existing or background IP?

### Source Code Escrow
- [ ] Is one party required to deposit source code into escrow?
- [ ] Are release conditions specified (e.g., bankruptcy, breach)?
- [ ] Is there a reference to an escrow agreement?

### Post-Termination Services
- [ ] Are there obligations after termination or expiration?
- [ ] Is there a transition or wind-down period?
- [ ] Are there survival clauses for confidentiality, indemnification, or limitation of liability?
- [ ] Is there a requirement to return or destroy materials?

## Evidence Extraction Rules

### License Grant
- **Keywords**: "license", "grant", "non-exclusive", "exclusive", "royalty-free", "right to use"
- **Location**: Typically in "License Grant" or "Grant of License" sections
- **Extraction**: Extract the full license grant clause, including scope, limitations, and any conditions

### Non-Transferable License
- **Keywords**: "non-transferable", "non-assignable", "non-sublicensable", "prior written consent", "assignment"
- **Location**: Often in "Assignment" or "Transfer" sections, or within the license grant itself
- **Extraction**: Extract the specific language prohibiting or restricting transfer, including any exceptions

### Affiliate License-Licensor
- **Keywords**: "affiliate", "subsidiary", "parent", "controlled", "on behalf of"
- **Location**: Within the license grant or definitions section
- **Extraction**: Extract language showing the license includes affiliates of the licensor or IP controlled by affiliates

### Affiliate License-Licensee
- **Keywords**: "affiliate", "subsidiary", "joint venture", "sublicense to affiliates"
- **Location**: Within the license grant or definitions section
- **Extraction**: Extract language showing the license extends to the licensee's affiliates

### Unlimited/All-You-Can-Eat License
- **Keywords**: "unlimited", "all you can eat", "enterprise", "unrestricted", "no limit"
- **Location**: Within the license grant or usage rights section
- **Extraction**: Extract the specific language granting unlimited rights, including any limitations

### Irrevocable or Perpetual License
- **Keywords**: "perpetual", "irrevocable", "survive termination", "in perpetuity"
- **Location**: Within the license grant or termination/survival sections
- **Extraction**: Extract the specific language describing the license as perpetual or irrevocable

### Source Code Escrow
- **Keywords**: "escrow", "source code", "deposit", "release", "escrow agent"
- **Location**: Often in a separate "Escrow" section or within "Software" or "Technology" sections
- **Extraction**: Extract the escrow clause, including deposit requirements and release conditions

### Post-Termination Services
- **Keywords**: "termination", "expiration", "transition", "wind-down", "survive", "return", "destroy", "sell-off"
- **Location**: Typically in "Termination" or "Effect of Termination" sections
- **Extraction**: Extract all post-termination obligations, including transition periods, survival clauses, and return/destruction requirements

## Boundary Rules

### What the skill SHOULD do:
1. **Identify license grants**: Look for explicit language granting rights to use IP, software, content, or marks.
2. **Detect transfer restrictions**: Identify clauses that limit or prohibit transfer, assignment, or sublicensing.
3. **Recognize affiliate involvement**: Identify when affiliates are included as licensors or licensees.
4. **Identify unlimited licenses**: Detect language granting unlimited or enterprise-wide usage rights.
5. **Identify perpetual/irrevocable licenses**: Detect language describing licenses as perpetual or irrevocable.
6. **Identify source code escrow**: Detect requirements to deposit source code with an escrow agent.
7. **Identify post-termination obligations**: Detect obligations that survive or arise after termination.
8. **Extract evidence**: Provide specific language from the contract as evidence.
9. **Flag for human review**: When language is ambiguous or requires interpretation.

### What the skill should NOT do:
1. **Do not interpret ambiguous language**: If the contract language is unclear, flag for human review.
2. **Do not assume implied licenses**: Only identify explicit license grants.
3. **Do not evaluate enforceability**: Focus on identifying clauses, not their legal validity.
4. **Do not assess business risk**: Do not evaluate whether terms are favorable or unfavorable.
5. **Do not modify contract language**: Extract and report, do not suggest changes.
6. **Do not infer from silence**: If a category is not mentioned, report "Not specified".
7. **Do not combine categories**: Each category should be evaluated independently.
8. **Do not provide legal advice**: The output is for informational purposes only.
9. **Do not exceed scope**: Only address the eight covered categories.
